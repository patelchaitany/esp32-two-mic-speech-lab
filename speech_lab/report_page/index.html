ESP32 Two-Mic Night Report

What a night of unattended experiments, plus held-out re-runs this evening, found about your two-mic recorder: how the board really behaves, how well the bus and meeting pipelines work in simulation and on real recordings, and what to change next.

Bottom line

  • Meeting: mostly works. On 8 held-out simulated rooms with 20 talkers each, the meeting pipeline raised the average talker's intelligibility (ESTOI) by +0.12 and the worst talker's by +0.06, close to the oracle's +0.07, and cut the level spread between talkers from 3.9 to 1.5 dB. The neural version slightly exceeds the noise-modulation limit; the classical version passes. On the real board the gain is about a third (+0.04 average).
  • Bus: only half works. Noise goes down (+5.4 dB SNR, worst partner +0.03 ESTOI on the held-out test), but nearby passengers stay as intelligible as before. With the mics 1.3 cm apart, direction can't tell a partner from a passenger, and voice-print extraction followed whoever was louder.
  • One bus setup worked on the board: Mic A's end pointed at the partners, with the axis beam and DPDFNet (+0.12 worst-partner ESTOI, passengers −8.6 dB). It needs the partners in line with the mics, and was only tested that way.
  • Calibrate at every boot. The mics are 1.3 cm apart, and Mic B is sampled one I2S frame (62.5 µs) late plus 0–7.5 µs that changes per boot. Self-calibration from 0.5 s of room noise handles it; the original calibration made results worse.
  • Moving the mics to 4 cm is optional: +0.004 ESTOI for the bus and +0.01 to +0.02 for the meeting.

Measured on the board

The board is not what the design assumed

Mic spacing≈ 1.3 cmPlausible 1.2–1.5 cm from acoustic fits; calipers will settle it. The design, the test bench and every tuned threshold assumed 4 cm.
Channel skew−62.5 to −70 µsMic B is sampled one I2S frame late, plus a fraction that is set at each boot: −1.10 samples at the first boot, −1.01 to −1.03 after 8 resets.
Largest acoustic delay±38 µsAt 1.3 cm. The skew is about twice that, so it must be removed before any spatial processing.
Mic B sensitivity−1.3 dBFor direct sound near the mic axis; −0.7 dB in diffuse noise. Across 8 boots it varied by 0.03 dB or less (std) at 0.5–2 kHz and 0.14–0.35 dB in other bands, so a factory constant works.
Room noise overnight−41 to −62 dBFSMostly the laptop fans under build load. Each mic has its own noise below 60 Hz at −35 dBFS, so the 80 Hz high-pass is required.
Capture chain0 droppedNo lost samples in any take. The ESP32 clock runs 17 ppm off the Mac's. Room RT60 is 0.4–0.5 s.

What calibration has to do

  • Remove the skew at every start. Take out the whole frame, then estimate the fraction from room noise: 0.5 s gave ±0.05 sample in this room. Speech from nearby talkers biases the estimate by +0.10 to +0.22 sample, so noise is the better source.
  • Store only what is fixed. Keep the integer frame, Mic B's gain and the caliper spacing in calib.json. Never store the fraction; it changes at each boot.
  • Why it matters. Left uncorrected at 1.2 cm, the skew puts the talker in the beam's null: −15 dB for a cardioid instead of +4.6 dB.
  • The original calibration could not do it. It only allowed skews of 0 or +0.5 sample and spacings of 1.5–8 cm, and returned zero skew on 16 of 16 board-like test scenes. The new self-calibration (x_s0v2) is within 0.06 sample on all 56 board recordings.
  • Settled tonight: the skew is a whole frame, not the half frame first assumed. A half-frame model needs 76–79 % of the room's noise to arrive from exactly one direction, and still fits worse.

Bus: keep the two partners, drop the bus

The bus goal is selective: keep the two partners, remove the bus and the other passengers. With the mics 1.3 cm apart, direction can't reliably separate a partner from a passenger, so the pipeline chosen on the tuning set uses no beam. It runs a neural denoiser (DPDFNet) on Mic A, remixes a −15 dB residual back in, and levels the result with an AGC gated on the partners.

Meeting: capture all twenty voices

The meeting pipeline cleans and levels everyone instead of picking talkers. It removes reverberation (WPE), steers a beam toward whoever is speaking, suppresses noise with a small neural network (GTCRN), and gives each speech segment its own gain so quiet and far talkers come up to the same level. Self-calibration runs first.

Physics + paired simulation

1.2 cm or 4 cm?

Not needed for the bus; a small gain for the meeting. The same simulated scenes were rendered at 12 mm and at 40 mm and run through the same methods, so the difference isolates the spacing.

  • Why 12 mm loses direction. Delays between the mics shrink to about 45 % of their true value in reverberant noise (85 % at 40 mm), so the direction tracker called the two bus partners inseparable in 9 of 10 layouts (6 of 10 at 40 mm).
  • Physics. 4 cm gives a beam about 1 dB more (+0.8–1.0 dB bus, +0.7–1.1 dB meeting), 3× finer direction resolution and about 6× less damage from the per-boot skew. 25 mm is within 0.1–0.3 dB of 40 mm.
  • Neural denoisers don't care. GTCRN on Mic A alone gained +0.024 / +0.026 at 12 / 40 mm.
  • Recommendation: keep 1.2 cm for now. If you revise the board, use 25–40 mm on one rigid strip, then calibrate; it helps the meeting and makes calibration more forgiving.
Real recordings, nothing replayed

Side experiments

These ran on audio already recorded from the board: LibriSpeech talkers played through the MacBook speakers, the room and fan noise, and the reset and drift takes. Each number comes from a JSON file in hw/realset/bizarre/; the write-up is hw/realset/BIZARRE.md.

Can two mics 1.2 cm apart tell a near talker from a far one?

The coherent-to-diffuse ratio separates talkers at 0.3–1 m from talkers at 2 m or more in quiet rooms, at 1.2 cm as well as at 4 cm. With diffuse noise at 0 dB it mostly measures SNR instead.

quiet: AUC 0.97 (1.2 cm), 0.96 (4 cm) · bus noise 0 dB: AUC 0.56

Can voice prints count the meeting talkers?

Speaker embeddings found all 8 talkers on clean audio. In the real fan noise the −12 and −15 dB talkers merged into others; with the fan noise 12 dB lower all 8 came back. Direction-based clustering found one cluster, because the speakers sit in only two places.

clean ARI 1.00 · real noise purity 0.72 · −12 dB noise purity 0.94–1.00

Does dereverberation rescue far talkers?

Two-channel WPE clearly helps far, reverberant talkers when the room is quiet, and almost nothing at realistic SNR. 60 filter taps were no better than 30.

ESTOI +0.21 / +0.23 noise-free far takes · +0.01…+0.03 real recordings · +0.13 at 30 dB SNR, +0.03 at 0 dB

Can a voice-print extractor pull out one partner from overlapping speech?

It extracts whichever partner is louder, and a wrong same-gender enrollment works almost as well. The quieter partner is not recovered with any enrollment.

louder: +9…+16 dB SIR, ESTOI +0.05…+0.20 · quieter: loses 6–29 dB

How stable is the calibration?

The skew is −1 sample plus a per-boot fraction: 0.11 at the cold first boot, 0.013–0.035 after each reset, steady within a boot. Mic B's gain does not change. Spacing fitted from noise wanders, so use calipers.

within a boot ±0.007 sample over 31 min · 0.5 s noise → ±0.05 · spacing from noise 1.06–1.99 cm

How much does an uncorrected skew hurt?

At 1.2 cm it destroys any beam: the talker falls into the null. At 4 cm the same skew only degrades it.

1.2 cm: −15 dB cardioid, −10 dB superdirective (should be +4.6 / +5.9) · 4 cm: +1.4 dB instead of +5.0

What is the most a fixed two-mic filter can gain on these recordings?

The real noise is partly incoherent between the mics at 150–500 Hz, which caps a 1.2 cm pair. Unloaded beams gain nothing on median; diagonal loading makes them reliably positive, close to the oracle ceiling.

oracle +2.5 dB median · loaded beam +2.0…+2.2 dB, all 18 takes positive · unloaded ≈ 0 dB (−5…+5)

Does the skew drift overnight?

Not measurably. Two sweeps from the same speaker 1.5 hours apart gave the same direct-sound delay. Noise-based estimates every 10 minutes until 06:25 wandered by about ±0.03 sample as the fan noise changed, inside the ±5 µs target. Only the first 1.5 hours are checked with a sweep.

direct path −0.516 sample at 01:55 and at 03:26 · self-calibration on 27 noise readings 02:05–06:25: −0.979…−1.032 (monitor's simpler fit −0.970…−1.042)

In this order

What to do next

  1. Tap test and calipers (5 minutes). While recording, tap next to the mic whose L/R pin goes to GND: channel 0 should jump by more than 10 dB. If channel 1 jumps, the labels are swapped and the one-frame skew comes from how the ESP32 pairs the I2S slots. Then measure the port spacing.Settles which mic is which and why Mic B is one frame late.
  2. Calibrate at every start. Use 0.5–1 s of room noise after each boot to estimate the skew; store only the integer frame, Mic B's gain and the caliper spacing. enhance.py already does this per file.Without it, the beams in these pipelines remove the talker instead of the noise.
  3. Record real people. A 3–5 minute meeting around a table, and a two-person conversation with other talkers nearby. Put talkers on Mic B's side and side-on to the board too; tonight's speakers sat only on Mic A's side.The real-board numbers rest on two speaker positions.
  4. Decide the bus approach. With this board, try bus-endfire with Mic A's end pointed at the partners. If both partners can't be in line with the mics, two mics 1.2 cm apart won't separate them from passengers; plan 3 or more mics, a wider array, or close-talk mics.This is the one requirement no method met in simulation.
  5. Add a speech-recognition test. Word error rate is still pending in the gates; it decides whether the neural denoisers help or hurt transcription.Intelligibility scores and transcription accuracy can disagree.

How to run it

Record with your record.py as before (left = Mic A, right = Mic B), then from the lab folder:

.venv/bin/python enhance.py ~/voice.wav -m meeting
.venv/bin/python enhance.py ~/voice.wav -m bus-endfire
afplay ~/voice_meeting.wav
  • Modes: meeting, meeting-classic, bus, bus-classic, bus-endfire. Each run prints the board calibration it measured from the recording; keep at least 0.5 s without speech in it.
  • Calibration only: .venv/bin/python -m methods.x_s0v2 file.wav.
  • Write-ups: hw/SPACING.md (board), hw/realset/RESULTS_REAL.md and BIZARRE.md (real recordings), study/SPACING_STUDY.md, study/final/BUS_V3.md and MEETING_V3.md (pipeline choices). Held-out results are in results3/final/test/.
So you can judge the numbers

How this was tested

  • Real board. ESP32-C3 with two INMP441 mics, 16 kHz, 24-bit over USB serial. LibriSpeech speech played from the MacBook speakers in clips of 4.2 s or less. A clean reference was fitted from each recording, so real takes can be scored. Bus noise and passengers were added digitally, because laptop speakers can't reproduce bus rumble. The build jobs kept the laptop fans running all night (−41 to −62 dBFS), so each real scene exists with the fans as recorded and 12 dB quieter.
  • Simulation. Simulated rooms with LibriSpeech speech and DEMAND noise. Bus: 30 tuning layouts and 30 held-out layouts at −5 and 0 dB SNR. Meeting: 20 tuning rooms and 8 held-out rooms with 20 talkers. Pipelines were chosen on the tuning sets only.
  • Scores. ESTOI intelligibility (0–1), SNR gain during speech, passenger suppression, level spread across talkers, each against Mic A with paired 95 % bootstrap intervals. Gates: G0 means do no harm, G1 means a meaningful gain.
  • Not done. No word-error-rate test (no speech recognizer downloaded), no listening panel, no real people. The overnight run stopped at 06:27 before its final tests; the held-out tests and the real-board runs of the final pipelines were run again this evening on the frozen code.
